USER: When was the MGB first announced and produced?  
ASSISTANT: The MGB was first announced on 19 September 1962, and production began that same year, continuing until 1980.

USER: What were the different body styles of the MGB?  
ASSISTANT: The MGB was initially a two-door soft-top roadster. Later variants included the MGB GT three-door 2+2 coupé from 1965 to 1980, the MGC six-cylinder sports car and coupé from 1967 to 1969, and the MGB GT V8 eight-cylinder 2+2 coupé from 1973 to 1976.

USER: How many MGBs were sold in total, including all variants?  
ASSISTANT: Sales for the MGB, MGC, and MGB GT V8 combined totaled 523,836 cars.

USER: What engine did the original MGB have, and how much power did it produce?  
ASSISTANT: The original MGB used a 1,798 cc B-Series engine that produced 95 hp (71 kW) at 5,400 rpm.

USER: When did the MGB get a five-bearing crankshaft?  
ASSISTANT: The MGB's engine was upgraded to a five-bearing crankshaft in October 1964.

USER: Why were US-market MGBs modified in 1975?  
ASSISTANT: In 1975, US-market MGB engines were de-tuned to meet emission standards, ride height was increased by an inch, and rubber bumpers were fitted to meet bumper regulations.

USER: What engine did the MGB use, and how did it change over the years?  
ASSISTANT: The MGB used the BMC B-Series engine, which was an enlarged version of the MGA's engine, increasing from 1,622 to 1,798 cc. Early models had a three-main-bearing crankshaft (18G-series), but in February 1964, a five-bearing crankshaft was introduced, changing the prefix to 18GB. Horsepower was rated at 95 net bhp until 1971, after which it gradually dropped due to emission regulations, reaching 85 bhp by 1975. US-spec cars saw a significant drop in power by the late 1970s, with some producing only around 70 hp.

USER: When did the MGB switch to a full synchromesh gearbox, and what was the new transmission based on?  
ASSISTANT: The MGB switched to a full synchromesh gearbox in 1968, replacing the earlier unit that had a non-synchromesh first gear. This new transmission was based on the MGC gearbox, designed to handle the higher output of the MGC's three-litre engine. It was over-engineered for the standard MGB but was used in the 3.5-litre V8 version of the MGB-GT-V8 as well. An automatic three-speed was also offered but remained unpopular.

USER: How did the overdrive system change in the MGB between 1968 and 1977?  
ASSISTANT: From 1968 to 1974.5, the MGB used a Laycock Type LH OD with a rectangular access cover and an oval clutch fork boot. In 1974.5, a revised version was introduced with a square clutch fork boot and a side fill plug instead of a dipstick. Overdrive operated in third and fourth gears until 1977, when it was limited to fourth gear only. The switch to engage overdrive was moved to the top of the gearshift knob in 1977.

USER: What were the differences between the early and later MGB transmissions?  
ASSISTANT: Early MGBs from 1962 to 1964 used a Laycock Type D OD with an external solenoid and a "shield" shaped access cover. From 1965 to 1967, a five-main-bearing engine led to changes in the input shaft, flywheel, and engine backing plate. The 1968 to 1974.5 models used a Laycock Type LH OD with a rectangular cover and oval clutch fork boot. The 1974.5 to 1980 models had a square clutch fork boot and a side fill plug, with the overdrive operating only in fourth gear after February 1977.

USER: What carburettors were used in the MGB, and how did they change for US models after 1975?  
ASSISTANT: All MGBs from 1963 to 1974 used twin 1.5-inch SU carburettors. After 1975, US-spec models switched to a single Stromberg 1.75-inch carburettor mounted on a combination intake–exhaust manifold. This change significantly reduced power and caused longevity issues due to the catalytic converter cracking the manifold. The compression ratio was also reduced to 8:1 on US-spec cars in 1972 to meet emission standards.

USER: How many MGBs were equipped with overdrive, and how was it engaged?  
ASSISTANT: Less than 20% of all MGBs were fitted with overdrive. It was available as an option on all models and could be engaged using a toggle switch on the dashboard or by pulling the wiper lever towards the driver, depending on the model. From 1977, the switch was moved to the top of the gearshift knob. Overdrive operated in third and fourth gears until 1977, after which it was only active in fourth gear.

USER: When did the MGB switch to a tube-type rear axle?  
ASSISTANT: The MGB GT first began using a tube-type rear axle in 1967, and all MGBs switched to this stronger axle design from 1968 onwards.

USER: What kind of brakes did early MGBs have?  
ASSISTANT: Early MGBs were fitted with 11-inch solid disc brakes on the front and drum brakes on the rear. The front brake calipers were made by Lockheed and used two pistons per caliper.

USER: When did MGBs get dual-circuit brakes?  
ASSISTANT: MGBs received dual-circuit brakes in 1968 to comply with US regulations. Before that, they used a single-circuit hydraulic system.

USER: When did power brakes become standard on the MGB?  
ASSISTANT: Servo assistance, or power brakes, were not standard on the MGB until 1975. Prior to that, stopping the car required significant pedal pressure.

USER: What was the electrical system like in early MGBs?  
ASSISTANT: Early MGBs had a simple electrical system with dash-mounted toggle switches for lights, fan, and wipers. The ignition switch was also on the dash, and the car used two 6-volt batteries in series for a 12-volt positive earth system.

USER: What changes were made to the MGB's electrical system later on?  
ASSISTANT: Later MGBs switched to a single 12-volt battery, changed from positive to negative earth, used safety-type toggle switches, replaced the dynamo with an alternator, and moved most controls to the steering column stalks.

USER: What were the key changes made to the MGB in 1968?  
ASSISTANT: In 1968, MG introduced the Mark II model with several updates. These included a new gearbox with synchromesh on all four gears, an optional Borg-Warner 35 automatic transmission (except in the US), a revised rear axle, and an alternator replacing the dynamo with a negative earth system. The new gearboxes also required a redesigned floorpan with a flat-topped transmission tunnel.

USER: Did the MGB have different body styles over the years?  
ASSISTANT: Yes, the MGB was primarily a two-seater roadster, with a small rear seat offered as a rare option. In 1973, a GT version was introduced with a more traditional-looking polished grille. Later, in 1979, limited-edition roadsters were produced in black for the US market, while the UK received bronze-painted roadsters and silver GT models.

USER: When did the MGB get its first major redesign after the Mark II?  
ASSISTANT: The MGB received a significant redesign in 1974, especially for the US market. The chrome bumpers were replaced with oversized rubber ones nicknamed "Sabrinas," and a new steel-reinforced black rubber bumper at the front incorporated the grille. This restyled the car's nose, and a matching rear bumper completed the change.

USER: What changes were made to the MGB in 1970 for the US market?  
ASSISTANT: In 1970, the US market MGB received split rear bumpers with the number plate in between. The car also got three windscreen wipers to meet safety regulations, high seat backs with head restraints, and side marker lamps. The front grille was changed to a recessed black aluminium version.

USER: How did British Leyland respond to US regulations in the mid-1970s?  
ASSISTANT: To meet US impact regulations, the 1974 US models had their chrome bumper over-riders replaced with oversized rubber ones. The front suspension was raised by 1 inch to compensate for low headlamps, but this, combined with heavier bumpers, led to poorer handling. In 1975, the front anti-roll bar was deleted as a cost-saving measure, though it remained available as an option.

USER: When did the last MGB roadster roll off the production line?  
ASSISTANT: The last MGB roadster was produced in late 1980 when the Abingdon factory closed. It returned to Abingdon County Hall Museum on 1 December 2011, lifted through a first-floor window and now on display in the main gallery. Production ended due to poor sales of the Triumph TR7 and a decision to stop cannibalizing its market.

USER: When was the MGB GT introduced and how long did it stay in production?  
ASSISTANT: The MGB GT was introduced in October 1965 and remained in production until 1980. Although it was still made until 1980, exports to the US stopped in 1974.

USER: What was special about the MGB GT's design and how did it improve performance?  
ASSISTANT: The MGB GT featured a greenhouse designed by Pininfarina, introducing a sporty "hatchback" style that combined a sloping rear window with a rear deck lid. This gave it the utility of a station wagon while keeping a coupe-like shape. Despite slightly slower acceleration due to added weight, its top speed improved by 5 mph to 105 mph because of better aerodynamics.

USER: How many MGB GT Jubilee models were made and what made them special?  
ASSISTANT: 751 MGB GT Jubilee models were produced in 1975 to celebrate the 50th anniversary of the MG Car Company. They were painted in pre-war British Racing Green, had tinted glass, gold body stripes, gold and black V8 alloy wheels, and other gold trim details.

USER: What engine did the MGC use and what were its performance figures?  
ASSISTANT: The MGC used a 2,912 cc straight-six C-Series engine with twin SU carburettors, producing 145 bhp at 5,250 rpm. It was capable of reaching 120 mph and could accelerate from 0–60 mph in 10.0 seconds.

USER: When was the MGB GT V8 introduced and what engine did it use?  
ASSISTANT: The MGB GT V8 was introduced in 1973 and used a 3,532 cc Rover V8 engine with an aluminium block and head. This engine produced 137 hp and 193 lb⋅ft of torque, allowing the car to reach 125 mph and accelerate from 0–60 mph in 7.7 seconds.

USER: Why was the MGC discontinued after only two years of production?  
ASSISTANT: The MGC was cancelled in 1969 after less than two years because the heavy engine and new suspension altered the handling, leading to a mixed response from the automotive press. It was also revealed that incorrect tyre pressures during the launch affected the car's performance.

USER: When was the MG RV8 produced and what engine did it use?  
ASSISTANT: The MG RV8 was produced starting in 1992, debuting at the British International Motor Show that October. It used a 3.9-litre aluminium Rover V8 engine, similar to the one found in the Range Rover.

USER: How many MG RV8s were made and where did most of them go?  
ASSISTANT: A total of 1,983 MG RV8s were produced. The majority were sent to Japan, with 1,579 units going there. The rest were sold in the UK and other markets, including a number reimported to the UK and Australia later.

USER: Did the MG RV8 have any left-hand-drive versions?  
ASSISTANT: No, the MG RV8 was only produced in right-hand-drive configuration. Although British Motor Heritage built seven left-hand-drive examples for US certification, they were never sold in the US and were instead sent to mainland Europe.

USER: What changes were made to the MG RV8 compared to the original MGB?  
ASSISTANT: The RV8 used only 5% of the original MGB parts, with 20% improved MGB components and 75% new parts. Notable changes include flared fenders, a new hood, Porsche 911-style headlights, and injection molded bumpers. It also featured an updated suspension system.

USER: Were any MG MGBs assembled in Australia, and if so, how many?  
ASSISTANT: Yes, the MGB was assembled in Australia from 1963 to 1972. Approximately 9,000 units were built during that time. All were roadsters and assembled from complete knock-down kits shipped from England.

USER: What were some of the motorsport successes of the MGB?  
ASSISTANT: The MGB achieved significant motorsport success, including a Grand Touring category win at the 1965 Monte Carlo Rally. It also won the Guards 1000 miles at Brands Hatch in 1965, the 84-hour Marathon de la Route at the Nürburgring in 1966, and the GT Category at the 1966 Targa Florio and Spa 1000.
