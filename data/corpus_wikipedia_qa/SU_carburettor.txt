USER: When was the SU carburettor invented and by whom?  
ASSISTANT: The SU carburettor was invented in 1904 by Herbert Skinner, a pioneer motorist and petrol engine developer. His younger brother, Carl Skinner, helped with its development and later became a key figure in the company that produced it.

USER: What happened to the SU carburettor company after World War I?  
ASSISTANT: After World War I, production resumed in 1918, but sales remained slow and the company was not profitable. In 1919, Carl Skinner sold the business to W. R. Morris, who became the owner and later moved production to the Wolseley factory in Birmingham.

USER: When did the S. U. Carburetter Company Limited become part of the Morris Organisation?  
ASSISTANT: The S. U. Carburetter Company Limited was incorporated on 15 September 1936 as part of the Morris Organisation, which later became known as the Nuffield Organisation.

USER: What was the original material used in the SU carburettor's design?  
ASSISTANT: The original SU carburettor used a leather bellows, which Herbert Skinner's son remembered his mother sewing. This was later replaced by a brass piston in later improvements.

USER: When was the SU carburettor first patented?  
ASSISTANT: Herbert Skinner applied for a patent in 1905, and it was granted in early 1906. He continued to develop and patent improvements through the 1920s.

USER: What happened to the SU carburettor company in the 1990s?  
ASSISTANT: The S. U. Carburetter Company Limited was voluntarily liquidated in December 1994. In 1996, the name and rights were acquired by Burlen Fuel Systems Limited, which formed a new company to continue manufacturing SU products.

USER: How does the SU carburettor control fuel delivery based on engine demand?  
ASSISTANT: The SU carburettor uses a sliding piston with a tapered needle that fits inside a fuel jet. As the piston moves up or down due to changes in airflow, the needle adjusts the open area of the jet, regulating fuel flow. This adjustment happens automatically based on the pressure drop in the venturi, which changes with engine demand. The result is a self-regulating system where fuel delivery matches air intake precisely.

USER: What is the purpose of the hydraulic dashpot in an SU carburettor?  
ASSISTANT: The hydraulic dashpot uses light oil to dampen the movement of the piston and prevent erratic changes. It resists upward movement more than downward, which helps enrich the fuel mixture temporarily when the throttle is suddenly opened. This acts like an accelerator pump, improving mixture richness during acceleration. Earlier versions before about 1955 did not have this dashpot.

USER: How does the SU carburettor achieve a constant depression in the venturi?  
ASSISTANT: The SU carburettor maintains a constant pressure drop in the venturi by adjusting the piston position. When airflow increases, the pressure drop rises, pushing the piston up to widen the venturi. When airflow decreases, the piston falls. This keeps the pressure drop constant regardless of airflow speed. The balance between the pressure difference and the spring force keeps the system stable.

USER: How does the SU carburettor differ from a slide carburettor?  
ASSISTANT: The key difference is how the piston is controlled. In a slide carburettor, the piston is directly connected to the throttle cable and moves based on rider input. In an SU carburettor, the piston moves indirectly due to airflow changes in the venturi. This means the SU adjusts fuel delivery based on engine demand rather than throttle position alone.

USER: What is used instead of a choke flap in an SU carburettor for cold starting?  
ASSISTANT: Instead of a choke flap, the SU carburettor uses a mechanism that lowers the jet assembly. This mimics the effect of the needle rising, increasing fuel supply to enrich the mixture. This system often includes a way to hold the throttle slightly open to prevent stalling. Some models also use a separate cold-start device for extra enrichment.

USER: Why is the needle profile important in an SU carburettor?  
ASSISTANT: The needle's tapered shape determines how fuel flow changes with the piston's position. Since the needle fits inside the fuel jet, its profile controls how much fuel is delivered at each venturi opening. The exact taper is tailored during engine development to match the engine's fuel needs precisely. This allows for better fuel metering than fixed-venturi carburettors.

USER: What are the different types of SU carburettors and when were they introduced?  
ASSISTANT: SU carburettors include several types introduced over the years: the "H" type in 1937, "HD" in 1954, "HS" in 1958, "HIF" in 1972, and earlier types like "HV" in 1929, "OM", and "KIF". The "HIF" type was used in the 1972–1974 MGB, while "H" and "HD" variants were used in earlier models. There were also larger versions like the 2-1/4" and 2-1/2" H models, now obsolete, and special Norman carburettors up to 3" in size.

USER: How do you determine the throat size of an SU carburettor from its model number?  
ASSISTANT: To find the throat size, look at the number at the end of the model designation. If it's a single digit, multiply it by 1/8" and add 1". For example, HS6 means 6/8" plus 1", which equals 1-3/4". If the number has two digits, it's the throat size in millimetres—like HIF38, which is 38 mm.

USER: Which cars used SU carburettors besides MG and Morris?  
ASSISTANT: SU carburettors were used in many British brands including Rolls-Royce, Bentley, Rover, Riley, Turner, Austin, Jaguar, Aston Martin, and Triumph. They were also used in Swedish Volvos and even on some aircraft engines like early Rolls-Royce Merlins. Later, they appeared in the Mini and Maestro through 1994.

USER: Were SU carburettors ever used on motorcycles?  
ASSISTANT: Yes, SU carburettors were popular on Harley-Davidson motorcycles as an upfit because of their compact side-draft design and ability to self-compensate for altitude changes. Many owners replaced stock carburettors with SU units until Keihin CV carbs became standard in 1990.

USER: Did any other companies make SU-style carburettors?  
ASSISTANT: Yes, Hitachi built carburettors under SU license, used on Datsun 240Z, 260Z, and others. While they looked similar, only the needles were interchangeable. Other companies like Stromberg, Ford/Motorcraft, Keihin, and Bing also developed similar variable venturi or constant vacuum designs, either licensed or modified to avoid patent issues.

USER: When did SU introduce their first electric fuel pump?  
ASSISTANT: SU introduced the Petrolift electric fuel pump in 1929, designed to replace vacuum-operated pumps. It was later superseded in 1932 by the L type fuel pump with a brass body and solenoid-operated diaphragm.
