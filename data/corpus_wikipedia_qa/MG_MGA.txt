ASSISTANT: The MGA was produced from 1955 until 1962. It was officially launched at the Frankfurt Motor Show in September 1955 and was replaced by the MGB after production ended in July 1962.

ASSISTANT: A total of 101,476 MGAs were produced by the end of July 1962. The majority were exported, with 5,869 sold in the home market.

USER: What engine did the original MGA have?  
ASSISTANT: The original MGA used a 1489 cc straight-4 engine from the BMC B series, initially producing 68 hp and later uprated to 72 hp. It was paired with a four-speed gearbox and a single H4-type SU carburettor.

USER: What was different about the MGA's body design compared to earlier MG models?  
ASSISTANT: The MGA featured a complete styling break with a streamlined ponton body that enclosed the full width and length of the car. It replaced the TF's articulated fenders and running boards, and had a more modern, integrated look.

USER: When was the Twin-Cam version introduced, and how many were made?  
ASSISTANT: The Twin-Cam version was introduced in 1958 and produced until April 1960. A total of 2,111 (or 2,210 according to some sources) were made before it was discontinued.

ASSISTANT: The Twin-Cam had a high-compression DOHC aluminium cylinder head producing 108 hp, four-wheel Dunlop disc brakes, and unique Dunlop peg drive knock-off steel wheels. It also featured a "Twin-Cam" badge on the bonnet.

ASSISTANT: A total of 31,501 MGAs were produced in less than three years before the Mark II version was introduced.

ASSISTANT: The 1961 Mark II MGA had a larger 1622 cc engine with a bore increase to 76.2 mm, a revised cylinder head, and increased horsepower to 90 bhp. It also featured a higher ratio 4:1 rear axle and an inset grille with Morris Mini-style tail lamps.

ASSISTANT: 8,198 Mark II roadsters and 521 coupés were built. There were also 290 Mark II De Luxe roadsters and 23 coupés produced.

ASSISTANT: The MGA has been raced extensively in the U.S., winning numerous SCCA regional and national championships. Kent Prather was especially successful, winning multiple G Production titles at the SCCA national championships between 1986 and 2005.
