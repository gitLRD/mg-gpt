ASSISTANT: The Austin Maestro was produced from November 1982 to December 1994. It was built in Oxford, England, at the former Morris plant in Cowley.

ASSISTANT: The Maestro was available in two body styles: a five-door hatchback and a two-door van. The hatchback was introduced first, while the van replaced the Morris Ital van derivative.

USER: When did the Maestro become a Rover?
ASSISTANT: The Maestro was sold under the Austin marque until 1988. After British Leyland became the Rover Group in 1986, the car was simply called the Maestro starting in 1988.

USER: What engine types were used in the Maestro?
ASSISTANT: The Maestro used A- and R-series engines. These were paired with a transmission bought from Volkswagen, marking a shift from the traditional Issigonis transmission-in-sump design used in earlier BL cars.

USER: What was the Maestro's drag coefficient?
ASSISTANT: The Maestro had a drag coefficient of 0.38 for most models. The HLE and MG versions had a slightly better coefficient of 0.36, thanks to rear strakes on the tailgate that improved airflow.

USER: How many Maestros were sold in total?
ASSISTANT: A total of 605,000 Maestros were sold during its twelve-year production run. After UK production ended, knock-down kits were assembled in Bulgaria and China in later years.

USER: What engine options were available in the original Austin Maestro lineup?  
ASSISTANT: The original Austin Maestro lineup included a 1.3-litre engine in the base, L, and HLE models, and a 1.6-litre engine in the L, HLS, and Vanden Plas versions. The MG Maestro also used the 1.6-litre engine.

USER: Did the Maestro have any unique features for its class when it was launched?  
ASSISTANT: Yes, the Maestro featured several pioneering elements for its class, including a bonded laminated windscreen, homofocal headlamps, body-coloured plastic bumpers made from polybutylene terephthalate, and an electronic engine management system. It also had a 12,000-mile service interval and white lamp indicators instead of the traditional orange.

USER: What special features came standard on the MG Maestro and Vanden Plas models?  
ASSISTANT: The MG Maestro and Vanden Plas models had solid-state instrumentation with a digital speedometer, vacuum fluorescent analogue displays for the tachometer, fuel, and temperature gauges, a trip computer, and a voice synthesis warning and information system. The Vanden Plas also featured bronze-tinted windows, power locks, and power windows for the front doors.

USER: When was the Austin Maestro launched and how did it perform in its early years?  
ASSISTANT: The Austin Maestro was officially launched on 1 March 1983, after production began in November 1982. It was Britain's sixth best-selling car in 1983 and 1984, with over 80,000 units sold in its second year.

USER: What kind of transmission did the HLE model have, and why was it chosen?  
ASSISTANT: The HLE model was fitted with Volkswagen's "monstrously long-geared" 3+E transmission, designed to maximize fuel economy, though it came at the cost of reduced performance.

USER: Did the Maestro have any features that were later used in other vehicles?  
ASSISTANT: Yes, the analogue instrument pod used in lower Maestro models was later adopted in the Range Rover starting in 1985. Additionally, the Maestro's body-coloured plastic bumpers were a first for the class, made from PBT and oven-cured at the same temperature as the car's steel body.
